Light sleepers

I stopped waking up at 5am. It wasn't my mattress.

I spent €1,400 on a new mattress, tried magnesium, banned my phone from the bedroom. The fix turned out to be the thing hitting my eyelids every summer morning.

LK
By Lena K. · 6 min read

For about three years, I woke up between 4:50 and 5:15 almost every morning from May to September. Not groggy. Fully, annoyingly awake. My partner slept straight through it.

I assumed it was stress. Then age. Then the mattress, which is how I ended up spending more on a bed than on my first car.

None of it changed the 5am wake-up. What finally did was embarrassingly simple, and I only found it because a sleep researcher on a podcast said one sentence I couldn't stop thinking about.

"Your eyelids are not curtains. Even closed, they let enough dawn light through to tell your brain the night is over."

Why the early light wins

Morning light is one of the strongest signals your body uses to decide it's time to wake. In summer, in most of Europe, the sky starts brightening before 5am. If any of that reaches your eyes, you're fighting your own body clock.

Blackout curtains help, but almost nobody's are perfect. There's the gap at the top, the edge by the wall, the standby light on the TV. I measured my "dark" bedroom with a light-meter app and found it nowhere near dark.

The three things I tried first (and why they didn't work)

  • Blackout curtains. Better, but light still came in around the edges.
  • A regular sleep mask. It pressed on my eyes, slid off by 3am, and leaked light at the nose.
  • Going to bed earlier. I just woke up at 5am with more sleep, then crashed by 3pm.

The mask that doesn't touch your eyes

The problem with flat masks is physics: to block light, they press against your eyelids, which is uncomfortable, so you loosen them, so they leak. A contoured mask works the other way. Two molded cups sit around your eyes, not on them, so it can seal at the edges without any pressure.

I ordered a Dimm Contour expecting to send it back. The first night, I woke up at 6:58, two minutes before my alarm, and genuinely didn't know what time it was. That had not happened in years.

Three months in, here's what I noticed:

  • No light leaks at the nose, which was where every other mask failed.
  • You can blink inside it. It sounds minor until you've worn a flat mask.
  • It stays on. I sleep on my side and it's still in place in the morning.
  • It works for travel too. Flights, hotel rooms, the sofa bed at my sister's.
